# Glowing hand-drawn/live-action fusion

Use for a single-space 15-second-style clip where a flat luminous hand-drawn entity touches the real world, continuously changes form, escapes, and is followed by a slightly late handheld camera. Adapt duration and ratio only when the user asks.

## Invariants

- The first 0–3 seconds show unmistakable contact with a real hand or object: wrapping a finger, landing in a palm, escaping a grasp, or emerging from a fingertip.
- It remains one entity throughout. Every new form retains a line, color, mark, or motion trace from the prior form; never introduce an unrelated replacement character.
- The action stays in one everyday space or adjacent connected areas so the person holding the phone can genuinely follow it.
- The camera reacts slightly late: the entity moves first, then the handheld camera catches up with plausible body movement and framing corrections.
- The maker/camera holder participates by reaching, catching, opening, stepping back, being teased, or revealing the next path.
- The tone is cute, lived-in, nostalgic, warm, or gently bittersweet—not horror or a jump scare.

## Five-beat construction

For a 15-second clip, use 0–3, 3–6, 6–10, 10–13, and 13–15 second beats. Each beat must add a new contact, transformation, movement, discovery, prank, or surprise while preserving continuity. End with a space-scale transformation across wall, floor, ceiling, window, sink, corridor, or another real surface, followed by an emotional aftertaste and a small comic detail.

Invent a fresh location, initial form, dominant color, transformation chain, contact mechanics, chase path, camera-holder reaction, and ending for each request. Do not reuse a stock chain mechanically.

## Material and camera language

Describe crayon, chalk, colored pencil, pastel, or rough brush texture; uneven fill; fibrous edges; slight line jitter; smudging; and frame-by-frame redrawing. Keep it visibly flat and handmade while it contacts real surfaces. Avoid polished neon vectors, plastic 3D CG, plush objects, seamless digital morphs, threatening mouths/teeth/eyes, attacks, sudden darkness, or teleportation.

Use live phone footage, imperfect stabilization, believable focus and exposure changes, small footstep/breath sounds, surface contact, paper/chalk-like strokes, room tone, and a delayed chase. Prefer one continuous shot; if a cut is essential, keep it within the same connected space and preserve the entity's exact state.

## Delivery and audit

Write an executable time-ordered prompt with space, five beats, material rules, camera behavior, exclusions expressed as positive target constraints where possible, and synchronized environmental sound. Verify early contact, single-entity continuity, a new event in every beat, delayed camera response, physical surface interaction, non-horror tone, and a space-scale ending.
